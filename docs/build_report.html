The claim being tested — and the answer

Can unsupervised harm-mechanism discovery over complaint narratives surface company–harm signals earlier than monitoring the CFPB's existing Product / Issue / Sub-issue taxonomy, without a higher false-alert rate?

The contribution is defined entirely as the gap to B1, the taxonomy baseline. If B1 wins, the honest headline is "the existing taxonomy is sufficient" — a real finding, and the README is written to say so. All four baselines have now run, and the gap is not there.

The spec says "fraction of members", so this was conformance, not tuning. Checked rather than claimed: recomputing all 2,821 production scores under the fix gives a largest difference of 0.00e+00. Zero scores moved — it cannot have been tuned toward an outcome it does not touch.

5

Signal detection complete

Disproportionality (PRR / ROR with empirical-Bayes shrinkage, ranked on EB05), EWMA control chart and PELT changepoint on the share series, Benjamini–Hochberg within product family. 49,935 signals, 20,664 at q ≤ 0.05.

The mandatory negative control failed at a 10.7% false-alert rate and found three bugs — one of them in the control itself. After the fixes it runs at 0.45% against an α of 0.05.

All three shared one signature: a quantity defined as "distinct groups" but computed as a sum over a partition — of companies in one case, of months in another. None is visible in a normal run; all three produce plausible, well-formed, entirely wrong numbers.

Top alerts are dominated by companies with real public enforcement histories — Lexington Law, Chime, Freedom Financial, Credit Karma, Coinbase. Checked for a structural artifact at the 2017 taxonomy boundary and found none: 1.5% of changepoints.

6gate

Ground truth & backtest gate passed

212 enforcement actions scraped and frozen before any signal existed; 112 usable after mechanical curation, spread across all eight years. All 8 annual cutoffs refit end-to-end.

The unavoidable methodological exposure, stated plainly. The candidate pool was frozen pre-detection and that is verifiable from git. But curation happened after Phases 4–5 ran, so the curator knew which companies alert. That cannot be undone — only bounded.

Both curation rules were pre-registered and committed before the code that applies them existed. The usable rule has no free parameter: no minimum-volume threshold, so a three-complaint company stays in and produces an honest miss. The bias audit compares resolved companies' alert share against a volume-matched expectation — 0.695 observed vs 0.675 expected, a 1.03× excess.

The refit found two real leaks before a single test was written. as_of and the signal date were read from the whole corpus rather than the data in scope, so a 2017 refit stamped its 367 clusters as_of 2026-08-03 and dated all 2,953 disproportionality signals after their own cutoff. EVALUATION §5 item 2 asserts the leakage guarantee on as_of — so that field being wrong silently voids the check meant to catch it.

Adjudication roughly halves both systems. Only 57% and 50% of company-level detections survive being asked whether the cluster describes the harm the order describes. That correction is far larger and far better supported than any difference between systems. At n = 8 the split looked like a reversal in HarmScope's favour, 5–3 with 2–0 discordant; six more actions took it to 8–7 with 2–1. The effect did not survive its own sample growing.

Three actions neither system can reach, for structural reasons. Bank of America's order concerns its own processing of garnishment notices; Citibank's is ECOA discrimination, which applicants are never told is the reason for denial; Santander's is a GAP add-on disclosure. Complaint narratives cannot contain what consumers do not know happened to them — a ceiling on the corpus, not on either method.

7result

Baselines all four complete

A baseline is a unit definition, not a parallel pipeline. Each system materialises its units into clusters under its own run_id, and every downstream stage runs over it untouched — panel, 2×2 margins, shrinkage, BH, EWMA, PELT, alert criteria, backtest. There is no second implementation that could diverge, which is a stronger reading of "same statistical machinery" than any refactor.

B2 is the row that carries the most weight, because it is the only comparison where granularity is not a confound. B2's topic count was fixed to HarmScope's own cluster count, per family per cutoff, before B2 had a detection rate — precisely so the artifact that produced B1's lead could not operate.

At the 2024 cutoff: 2,010 units against 2,010, median support 16 against 15, clearing the floor 54.4% against 51.6%. Matched on every dimension the threshold artifact works through, a bag-of-words topic model detects 58 actions to the embedding pipeline's 57 — 54 of them the same actions, discordant 4–3, p = 1.00. A bug caught before it became a result. TfidfVectorizer l2-normalizes by default, leaving each document with about one unit of pseudo-count mass — and LDA fit on that has essentially no data. Measured at credit_reporting, k=195: 5 of 195 topics ever won an argmax, one holding 99.0% of documents. Unchanged at max_iter 5/20/50 and unchanged with use_idf=False, so normalization was the cause, not weighting or convergence.

With norm=None all 195 populate and the largest holds 5.4%. The first B2 run produced 114 units where HarmScope has 1,101 — a baseline crippled by an implementation detail would have read as evidence against bag-of-words topic models.

Cost note: B3's 2024 cutoff took 516 minutes on credit_reporting alone. BERTopic's min_cluster_size=10 against HarmScope's 50 produces 8,363 clusters on a 500k fit sample, and HDBSCAN's cost follows.

Pulled forward — does the encoder choice matter?

The README carried a "provisional, dev model" flag since Phase 3, with a full bge-base re-encode as the implied fix: 16.8 h plus a re-run of Phases 4–7. Rather than pay that to find out whether it changes anything, the question was scoped to what one family can answer. 150,000 credit_reporting representatives at the 2024 cutoff, encoded on both models, clustered with identical config. Two guards made this readable, and both mattered.

The cross-encoder ARI is meaningless without its within-encoder baseline. 0.232 alone looks like proof of anything you like. Against a within-encoder disjoint-halves ARI of 0.469 on the same sample — reproducing the 0.505 already recorded — the honest statement is that the encoder disagrees about twice as much as resampling does.

assign_max_distance = 0.35 is calibrated to MiniLM's geometry. bge sits on a visibly higher cosine scale, so its 100% assignment at a fixed 0.65 floor is the threshold moving, not coverage improving. Reporting that as coverage would have reproduced the support-floor artifact one entry after diagnosing it.

Verdict. Cluster identity is encoder-sensitive, so the provisional flag was justified and nothing may treat a cluster as the same object across encoders. Cluster granularity is not, at 4% apart — and granularity is what drives the Phase 7 table. The swap is unlikely to move the ordering. The full encode stays outstanding as a whole-table item, since a cross-system comparison needs every system on one encoder.

Validated on a positive control first: with the bge matrix replaced by a copy of MiniLM's, cross-encoder ARI is 1.000 and within-encoder halves 0.499 against the recorded 0.505. ARI throughout is computed by src.cluster.stability — the same held-out protocol and noise-excluding ari() that produced 0.505, because two ARIs computed differently are not comparable and that comparison is the entire point.

The threshold sweep — why no ordering here is stable

ROADMAP Phase 9 requires a sensitivity analysis on every threshold in config.py. The one that gates an alert — min_supporting_groups, frozen at 15 — was swept because the Phase 7 headline turns on it.

The ordering changes sign three times, and the frozen value sits in the band that favours B1. The mechanism is granularity, measured rather than asserted: HarmScope splits the same complaints into 2,010 units against B1's 634, so its support-per-unit distribution sits lower by arithmetic — median 15 against 18, clearing the floor 51.6% against 57.3%.

The threshold was not moved. It was frozen before the backtest, and changing it after seeing which system it favours is exactly the trap. The real defect is that an absolute support floor is not comparable across systems of different granularity; the fix is a floor expressed as a quantile of each system's own distribution — recorded as future work, not applied retroactively.

The leakage guard is the one worth dwelling on. EVALUATION §5.6 calls it "the one most likely to be violated by accident" — it is the check that would catch changing a detection threshold after seeing which system it favours. It counted rows in backtest_results; the harness writes baseline_results. The count was always zero, so the "no backtest yet" branch always ran.

It now compares CONFIG.signals field by field against the config each backtest run recorded in its own params. Field by field rather than on the whole-config fingerprint, because a Phase 8 LLM setting would trip a fingerprint comparison while changing no threshold — and a check that cries wolf is how this one died in the first place.

Determinism is exact. Two consecutive runs on identical input: 0 panel rows and 0 signal rows differ — from 1,114 and 81 before.

Honest limitations

  • The headline is a null result. HarmScope is indistinguishable from B1, B2 and B3, and beats only B0. That is a real finding and the README leads with it rather than with the sample size at which it looked otherwise.
  • The one thing the pipeline demonstrably buys is the gap to naive volume — +9.8 points, p = 0.035. Everything above that line is within noise.
  • The adjudicator is the process that built the system. Worklists blind rank, statistic and decoy status, and order is seeded — but the adjudicator has seen the alert list, which no worklist design fixes. Recorded as claude-opus-5, never as human labelling, and the intra-rater check is not yet done.
  • n = 14 of 112 for adjudication. The corrections are estimates from a small paired sample; the intervals are wide and stated as such.
  • Phases 3–7 all ran on the dev model. The encoder experiment bounds what that costs but does not eliminate it: a full bge-base encode is ~16.8 h and must move the whole table at once, since a cross-system comparison needs every system on one encoder.
  • Curation post-dates detection. Bounded by pre-registration and a bias audit, but a permanent asterisk — and the anti-leakage test for it is written to fail rather than be pointed at the wrong file.
  • Two absolute thresholds are known not to be comparable across systems: min_supporting_groups across granularities, and both it and min_a across B3's complaint-denominated units. The quantile fix is recorded, not applied.
  • Complaint volume is not harm volume. No denominator of customers exists; reporting propensity varies by product, company size and third-party filing activity.
  • A process failure worth naming: shell && chains twice reported tail's exit code instead of the real one, hiding a failed install and a failing test.
